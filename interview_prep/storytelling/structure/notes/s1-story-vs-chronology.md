# S1 — Story vs. chronology

## Mental model

**A chronology answers "what happened." A story answers "what did you do when it wasn't obvious what to do."**

Three load-bearing parts. Everything else is optional detail.

1. **Tension** — a specific thing that could have gone wrong, or two things you couldn't have both. Deadline vs. correctness. Ship vs. migrate. Your read vs. the lead's read. No tension → no story, just a status report.
2. **Decision** — the fork *you* stood at, and which way you went. If a listener can't name the moment you chose, they heard a process description, not a story.
3. **Consequence** — what changed because of the decision. Ideally a number (impact track). Minimum: a hard, checkable outcome.

Compact form: **"X was true, which forced a choice between A and B; I chose A because C; result was D."**

## Why it works

Interviewers are scoring *judgment under incomplete information*, because that's the job. A chronology gives them no evidence of judgment — the events would have happened to anyone in the seat. A decision under tension is the only part of your account that is uniquely attributable to you.

Secondary effect: tension holds attention. A listener tracking "will this work out?" retains the details. A listener tracking a timeline is filing, not listening.

## Practical consequences

- Start from the **decision**, not from the beginning of the project. Find the fork first, then work backwards to the minimum setup needed for the fork to make sense.
- The setup ("Situation") exists only to make the tension legible. Everything in it that doesn't create pressure gets cut.
- "We migrated to X and it went well" is not a story. "The migration had to happen during the payment freeze window, so I had to pick between a dual-write with reconciliation risk and a 4-hour outage" is.
- Multi-decision projects hold multiple stories. Pick one fork per answer.

## Failure modes at this stage

| Symptom | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Answer is a timeline: "first we... then we... then we..." | No fork identified |
| Everything went smoothly | You picked an event with no tension, or you sanded it off |
| Listener asks "so what was hard about it?" | Tension was implied, never stated |
| You describe the system more than your choices | Deep-dive-tour failure mode (see F4) |
| The decision was your lead's / the team's | Wrong event for a behavioral answer, or wrong framing of your part |
